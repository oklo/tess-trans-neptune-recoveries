Project/debug overview
- This pass used Pluto as the bright real-data calibration case for the stack-alignment path.
- The goal was not to make a Pluto science claim. The goal was to determine whether the current offset behavior comes from the stacker itself, from track/pixel conventions, or from support/interpolation artifacts.
- Sedna and Pluto both showed a near-nominal offset preference in earlier work, so Pluto was treated as the frozen debug case before returning to Sedna.

What was audited
- Explicit shift math and axis order
- Measured-centroid stacking on a bright 32-cadence Pluto subset
- Integer-shift vs subpixel-shift behavior
- Contribution/support maps
- Frame-by-frame nominal vs nearby-offset aperture photometry
- Real-bundle injections on the frozen Pluto subset
- Rerun of the public Pluto recovery/null-control path after a small safe recovery change

Shift-formula / axis-order findings
- The stacker uses the expected image-coordinate shifts:
  - dx = x_ref - x_i
  - dy = y_ref - y_i
- The image sampler is fed shifts in array-axis order:
  - axis0 shift = dy
  - axis1 shift = dx
- This was checked explicitly in code, logged in the audit summary, and guarded by a targeted unit test.
- Real-bundle nominal injections also land at the intended reference pixel, which argues against a gross sign or axis-order bug in the stacker implementation itself.

Measured-centroid stack result
- I selected the 32 brightest Pluto cadences by nominal-track moving-aperture S/N for centroid diagnostics.
- Geometry still passes cleanly on the frozen bundle:
  - corner residual median/max = 0.00445 / 0.00594 px
- On this bright subset:
  - nominal subpixel stack aperture S/N = 18.53
  - measured-centroid stack aperture S/N = 21.90
  - nominal track with x offset of -0.5 px gives aperture S/N = 26.08
- This means the stacker can form a compact source near the intended reference location on real Pluto data.
- Therefore the stacker is not fundamentally broken in the sense of “cannot align a real moving source at all.”

Integer vs subpixel shift result
- On the same bright Pluto subset:
  - nominal subpixel stack aperture S/N = 18.53
  - nominal integer-shift stack aperture S/N = 11.35
- Integer shifting behaves worse than the current subpixel path, not better.
- This argues against subpixel interpolation being the primary root cause.
- Interpolation/support details still matter, but the main failure does not look like “linear interpolation is fundamentally mis-shifting the source.”

Support-map findings
- Support maps were generated for the nominal, x-offset, and centroid subset stacks.
- At the fixed scoring location, support is full for all three variants:
  - support_at_reference = 32 / 32 frames
- The bright-source preference on the subset is therefore not caused by missing coverage in the scoring aperture.
- Global maxima can still appear on edges in some stack visualizations, but those edge peaks are not what drives the fixed-position aperture S/N used for the main comparison here.

Frame-by-frame nominal vs nearby-offset findings
- Full-bundle frame-by-frame offset photometry does not show a single clean winning offset across all cadences.
- The exact nominal track wins only 40 / 864 frames.
- Best offsets are spread broadly around the nominal track, with the most common winners on the full bundle being:
  - (-0.5, +0.5): 139 frames
  - (-0.5, -0.5): 126 frames
  - (+0.5, -0.5): 125 frames
  - (+0.5, +0.5): 105 frames
- This means there is not a single simple full-bundle constant offset that explains every cadence.
- However, on the bright subset the measured centroids show a consistent negative x residual:
  - median centroid residual = (-0.242, -0.012) px
  - mean centroid residual = (-0.290, +0.088) px
- The brightest-subset behavior therefore points to a small x-side track/convention mismatch even though the full bundle is not cleanly described by one constant offset.

Injection findings
- Injecting a synthetic moving PSF directly into the real Pluto bundle subset works as expected.
- A nominal-track injection produces a very strong compact stack at the reference location:
  - injection nominal aperture S/N = 196.46
  - peak at (10, 10)
- An injected source offset by +0.5 px in x also behaves physically:
  - injection offset aperture S/N = 177.03
  - peak at (10, 10)
- These injection results strongly support the conclusion that the stacker itself is functioning correctly on real-bundle data.

Public-pipeline rerun after the small safe fix
- I made one safe recovery-path fix:
  - when a real cube bundle already provides local_x/local_y, recovery now prefers those exact bundle-local coordinates over re-deriving local coordinates from the interpolated detector track
- This removes a small avoidable interpolation seam in real-data recovery and changes the reported track coordinate source to cutout_local_bundle.
- It does not solve the main Pluto behavior by itself.
- After the fix, the frozen full-bundle Pluto recovery with background none and interpolation linear gives:
  - nominal score = 2.7999
  - best local score = 3.9364 at (0.0, +1.0)
- Null controls remain problematic:
  - best control score = 3.9364 at (0.0, +1.0)
  - nominal minus best control = -1.1365
- So Pluto nominal still does not beat nearby controls cleanly on the full 864-cadence bundle.

Root-cause conclusion
- The evidence does not support “the stacker implementation itself is wrong.”
  - Toy axis-order test passes.
  - Real-bundle injections stack correctly.
  - Measured-centroid stacking on a bright Pluto subset produces a compact source at the intended location.
- The strongest primary issue is upstream of the core stacker:
  - there is a real small x-direction mismatch between the predicted local track and the bright-subset Pluto centroids
  - on the brightest subset, a simple x = -0.5 px adjustment outperforms both the nominal track and the measured-centroid stack
- A full symmetric half-pixel convention bug is not proven.
  - The evidence is stronger for an x-side convention or track mismatch than for a generic +/-0.5 px bug in both axes.
- A secondary issue remains on the full bundle:
  - long-run recovery scores are still vulnerable to residual systematics/null-control structure
  - even after the safe fix, the exact nominal Pluto track does not beat nearby controls cleanly

Whether Pluto nominal now behaves physically
- On the bright subset: mostly yes
  - the source is compact at the intended reference location
  - centroid and x-offset comparisons are physically interpretable
- On the full 864-cadence bundle: not yet
  - the nominal track still loses to nearby null controls
  - the best local offset remains displaced from the exact nominal track

Implications for Sedna
- It is not yet reasonable to return to Sedna as if the stack-alignment path is fully settled.
- The audit says the core stacker is usable, which is important progress.
- But Pluto nominal still does not behave cleanly enough across the full frozen bundle to treat Sedna’s near-nominal offset behavior as purely sensitivity-limited.
- Sedna should wait until the Pluto nominal or explicitly corrected track beats nearby controls cleanly on the bounded real bundle.
