Pluto Catalog-Mask Stack And Sedna Trial
2026-04-08

Context
- This follow-up applied the same simple raw-frame source-masking workflow to:
  - the retained full-duration Pluto Sector 92 / camera 2 / CCD 1 bundle
  - the trusted bounded Sedna Sector 5 / camera 1 / CCD 4 barycentric bundle
- The goal was not precision photometry. The goal was to test whether a rough,
  physically transparent masking plus local background subtraction is already
  enough to make the target visible in a nominal-track raw-frame stack.

Workflow
- Start from the existing detector-local NPZ bundle and its local track.
- Query TIC once for the detector patch and keep the bright subset that enters
  the retained 21x21 cutout.
- Assign a simple circular mask radius based on Tmag.
- Preserve a fixed central 5x5 region so the target core is not masked away.
- For each raw frame:
  - mask the bright catalog sources outside the central 5x5 region
  - subtract the mean of the remaining unmasked non-central pixels
- Shift and stack on the nominal moving-target track.

Code paths
- scripts/list_pluto_catalog_crossers.py
- scripts/run_pluto_catalog_mask_stack.py

Pluto result
- Bundle:
  outputs/pluto_full_duration_conservative_mask/pluto_sector92_cam2_ccd1_full_cube_bundle_masked.npz
- Catalog crossers:
  784 raw entries, 778 unique Gaia/TIC source keys, 63 bright sources masked
- Simple nominal raw-frame stack result:
  - center aperture S/N = 57.53
  - brightest off-center peak still exists at (20, 15)
  - but Pluto is now a clear compact central source in the nominal stack
- Interpreted conservatively, this rough workflow already retains an order-unity
  fraction of Pluto's expected signal. It is not just recovering a tiny residual.

Sedna result
- Bundle:
  outputs/real_control_study/barycentric/sedna_sector5_cam1_ccd4_barycentric_cube_bundle.npz
- Catalog crossers:
  80 unique Gaia/TIC source keys, 10 bright sources masked
- Simple nominal raw-frame stack result:
  - center aperture S/N = 2.26
  - brightest peak remains off-center at (19, 12)
  - the central Sedna position is lifted modestly, but not cleanly enough to
    count as a comparable recovery to Pluto

Interpretation
- The same rough-and-ready masking/background subtraction absolutely works as a
  Pluto calibration demonstration.
- The same workflow does not yet rescue Sedna.
- That is consistent with the current scientific picture:
  Pluto is bright enough that a transparent raw-frame nominal-track stack can
  reveal it once obvious catalog contaminants are reduced, while Sedna remains
  close enough to the residual noise/systematics floor that a more careful
  treatment is still needed.

Next useful step
- Use the raw-frame Sedna stack plus the Sedna bright-source list to inspect the
  central 5x5 versus the dominant off-center structures cadence by cadence
  before deciding whether further masking, frame rejection, or track/local-WCS
  cleanup is the better next move.
