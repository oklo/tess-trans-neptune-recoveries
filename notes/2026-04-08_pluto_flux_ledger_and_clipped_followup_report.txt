What the official/simple Pluto light curve showed
- The official Pluto light curve is not a clean "problem solved" result.
- Both the aperture and PSF series show a large late-time ramp in the final
  part of the frozen two-day window.
- That ramp is not interpreted as real Pluto brightening.
- The safer interpretation is a broad late-time background or stray-light
  systematic leaking into the official source estimate.

Why the late ramp is treated as contamination
- Pluto remains near the cutout center, so this is not an edge-crossing effect.
- In the official aperture table, the background term climbs sharply late in
  the run.
- In the official PSF table, the fit quality also worsens late in the run.
- The transparent local moving-aperture series changes much less than the
  official source estimates over the same cadences.
- Taken together, those checks argue for broad systematics contamination rather
  than real target variability.

- This is substantially more physical behavior than the earlier full-window
  off-axis preference.

How to interpret the current Pluto state
- Pluto is present in the frozen bundle.
- The official high-level tess_asteroids workflow now works here.
- The frozen two-day window is still contaminated at late times by broad
  systematics.
- The contaminated tail was materially hurting the full-window Pluto
  shift-stack exercise.
- After clipping that tail, the bounded background=none Pluto recovery behaves
  much more physically.
- This does not mean Pluto is fully solved in every configuration, but it does
  make the bounded debug case cleaner and more trustworthy.

What remains uncertain

- The broader question of how robustly the custom stack path behaves outside
  the clipped window still needs follow-up.
